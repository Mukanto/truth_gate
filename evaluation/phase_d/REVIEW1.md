### R1-1 (blocking): Arm R repeats V1/V2, and the main result mostly measures the world author

- **Section:** §2.2, §2.3, §5 Run, §8 I-D3 and I-D6, §9 item 4, §0.2 last paragraph, §0.3 Main
- **Passage:** "The reference world is used for: the validity checks; the descriptive arm R." / I-D3: "arm R near the ceiling but W-1 failing: the world author's coverage stays the bottleneck even with the checklist."
- **Reason:** Arm R runs the gate on the same reference worlds that V1 and V2 used to select the pairs. V1 requires the faulty criterion to hold in at least 0.25 of reference `minus` samples. The gate raises K2_HIGH_SIZE from about 16 to 20 of 200 (0.08 to 0.10, depending on the Bonferroni family). So every valid pair is detected in arm R by construction. V2 likewise fixes the fixed twin's `minus` and noise streams. If validity_check.py uses the gate's seeds (the draft says only "fixed seeds"), V1 and V2 compute arm R's `minus` and noise outcomes exactly, before the freeze (PROTOCOL 1.11). The known part is also not separated from the prospective one (PROTOCOL 1.2). Consequences: (i) I-D3's first pattern is decided in advance whenever W-1 fails. Open decision 4 ("arm R against W-1 shows whether they are needed") rests on a ceiling that is selected, not measured. (ii) "Arm R itself failing" can only come from streams that V1/V2 do not check. Yet reference.py as specified has only `plus` and `minus` and no control, so every arm-R study gets K4_NO_CONTROL (mandatory), and every arm-R fixed twin fails clean pass unless someone adds controls; the draft does not say who. (iii) Beyond V1, the confirmatory W-1 result mostly measures whether the W-1 world author planted the structure strongly enough. Once the faulty criterion holds in `minus` above about 0.09 the flag is automatic, and the only world-independent part of the gate is the noise probe. §0.2's "Phase D separates them" therefore does not hold for the confirmatory result, and §0.3's "does Truth_Gate 0.5.0 flag ..." credits the gate with what is mostly the world author's coverage.
- **Fix:** (1) §2.3: "validity_check.py draws its samples from a seed stream disjoint from the gate's (base seed stated, different from Budget.base_seed); nobody computes a gate stream of a Phase D file before the case freeze." (2) Choose one: report arm R as a "consistency check, expected at least as high as V1 by construction" and drop the word "ceiling"; or make arm R informative by running it on the worlds of a second, independent structure author, which V1/V2 never saw (same inputs, controls written under PC world-instruction rule 4). (3) §2.2: "reference.py declares at least one control (PC world-instruction rule 4) and returns exactly the brief's keys, shapes and dtypes." (4) §0.3 Main: "Does the system Truth_Gate 0.5.0 plus a world author given the checklist flag ...; the share that needs no declared world (the noise probe) is reported separately." (5) Give I-D3's arm-R patterns numeric definitions (for example "arm R attributable detection at least 18 of 20"). Make §9 item 4's recommendation depend on the independent arm R only, or delete it.

### R1-2 (blocking): The primary endpoint is defined twice, incompatibly, and the margin rejects real detections

- **Section:** §5 Attributable detection; §10 last bullet
- **Passage:** "the faulty criterion holds at least 20 more times out of 200 than the fixed criterion of the same arm; the difference has a one-sided Fisher exact p below 0.05." / §10: "`attributable.py` must reproduce the strict reading of PC section 9 on B2-08 to B2-11."
- **Reason:** The two passages contradict each other. On PC's own numbers (REPORT_PHASE_C 7.3), the §5 rule makes B2-08 (`minus` 86 against 47), B2-10 (89 against 31) and B2-11 (132 against 46) attributable through K2_HIGH_SIZE, and B2-09 attributable through the noise flag (67 against 25). The strict reading of PC §9 counts all four as detected without the error. So the tool either fails its acceptance test or is bent to pass it before the freeze. Underneath is a choice the draft leaves open. §5 asks whether the error raises the rate on a flagged stream. §0.2 (D-3) and the strict reading ask whether the flag would fire without the error. The margin is also miscalibrated. At n = 200 the Fisher condition is redundant: for any fixed count b, a faulty count of b + 20 gives p ≤ 0.029, so the rule is a pure 20-count margin. With the fixed twin at its nominal 10 of 200, attribution needs at least 30 of 200, while the gate flags from about 17 to 19. A flagged detection with a faulty count of 18 to 29 and a fixed twin that is not flagged on that stream is rejected, although the error alone explains it. Chance attribution is small: with equal true rates I simulated at most 0.025 per stream at a rate of 0.5, and 0.008 at 0.2. The rule therefore errs toward rejecting real detections, not toward accepting spurious ones.
- **Fix:** Replace the rule with: "A detection is attributable in an arm when the faulty study's verdict is NOT_FALSIFIABLE and at least one of its expected rate flags, on a stream s, (a) is not carried by the fixed study of the same arm on s, and (b) has a faulty count on s above the fixed count with a one-sided Fisher exact p < 0.05." Report the present 20-count rule as a secondary scoring. Before the coder starts, the designer computes from the PC certificates how every Phase C B-2 and B-3 detection is classified under the chosen rule, and writes that list into §10 as the fixture. Whether B2-08 to B2-11 come out non-attributable under (a) depends on their control and noise streams; the report alone does not settle it (unclear).

### R1-3 (major): I-D1 misdescribes the pairs that fail V1

- **Section:** §2.4 drop labels; §8 I-D1 first bullet
- **Passage:** "A pair that fails V1 has an error that never makes its criterion hold where the claim is false at that size. A check of decisions, which the gate is, cannot detect such an error by design. ... Their rate per subtype is reported as a limit of the gate"
- **Reason:** V1 requires the faulty criterion to hold in at least 0.25 of reference `minus` samples, and at least 0.15 more often than the fixed one. A pair whose faulty criterion holds in 0.10 to 0.24 fails V1, yet the gate would flag it (K2_HIGH_SIZE from about 0.085 to 0.10). For such pairs both the I-D1 sentence and the drop label "error does not manifest at a feasible size" are false. The V1-failure rate also depends on the structure author's world, the criterion author's implementation, the claim's threshold and the repair choice, so it is not "a limit of the gate". Finally, the sentence settles the auditors' disagreement (tool limit against case size) by decree, without the opposite reading that PROTOCOL 1.4 requires.
- **Fix:** Split V1 failures by the faulty rate in reference `minus`. (a) At or below the gate's flag threshold for that study, which validity_check.py computes from the study's Bonferroni family: "error does not change decisions at this size". (b) Above that threshold but below 0.25, or without the margin: "error manifests below the V1 bar (excluded by design)". Reword I-D1: "The rate of (a) per subtype estimates how often a documented error, as built by these agents at these sizes, changes no decision. It describes the scope of any decision-level check, not a measured limit of this gate. The opposite reading (Phase B auditor) is that an outcome-level check that cannot see an inflation below the threshold is a tool limit; both readings are reported." Alternatively, set V1 to "faulty count above the flag threshold and Fisher p < 0.01 against the fixed count", and keep 0.25 only as a reported band.

### R1-4 (major): W-1 clean-pass failures are not "false alarms of the gate"

- **Section:** §8 I-D3 third bullet; §4; §6 H-D1/H-D2
- **Passage:** "clean-pass failures on pairs that passed V2: genuine false alarms of the gate, each reported with its flags."
- **Reason:** V2 checks the fixed criterion only in the reference world and on noise. The checklist tells W-1 authors to put time order, groups, shared components and many variables into both worlds. Meanwhile §2.4 forbids giving the fixed criterion "a rule that the claim does not state". A fixed criterion that corrects only the documented error (an ordinary t test, a random split) will then hold too often in W-1 `minus`. That is the mechanism of PC's B3-12, an ordinary t test on autocorrelated months. The gate's flag on such a criterion is correct, so calling it a "genuine false alarm of the gate" is wrong. A real false alarm is a flag on a stream where the fixed criterion's true rate is at or below the declared alpha, which happens at about alpha_family per study. The checklist may therefore lower W-1 clean pass for reasons outside the gate. H-D1 and H-D2 then score these as gate failures, and no reading in §8 covers the case.
- **Fix:** Replace the bullet with a mechanical classification of every failed clean pass, per arm, from the certificate: "fixed criterion not clean in the declared worlds (the flagged stream's rate is above the flag threshold; a correct flag)" against "possible false alarm (the flagged rate's 95% Wilson interval includes the declared alpha)". Add a reading: "W-1 clean pass below W-0 clean pass: the checklist adds dependence that the documented corrections do not handle; this is a limit of the checklist, not of the gate." Add the clean-pass guard of R1-5 to the adoption rule.

### R1-5 (major): Two adoption rules, no clean-pass guard, incomplete outcome map

- **Section:** §4 last paragraph; §8 I-D2 and I-D3; §6 H-D3
- **Passage:** §4: "it is adopted only if H-D3 holds (section 8)" / I-D2: "If H-D1 or H-D2 holds and H-D3 holds, then ... The checklist then goes into the methods protocol"
- **Reason:** These are two different adoption rules. Neither looks at clean pass, although the checklist can raise detection while lowering clean pass (R1-4), and H-D3 tests detection only. Add a table with one reading for each of the 8 combinations of H-D1, H-D2 and H-D3. Define the arm-R patterns numerically. Report the discordant counts per class (descriptive), and state that H-D3 is a pooled claim that does not support a checklist effect within a single class.

### R1-6 (major): The checklist names this set's mechanisms; the claims wording understates it

- **Section:** §4 (checklist and its last paragraph); §8 I-D4, I-D5
- **Passage:** "The checklist names kinds of dependence, not errors." / I-D4: "Allowed: ... a model of a different Claude line that was not told the error, with or without a checklist of dependence kinds."
- **Reason:** The four items map one to one onto the errors in this set. Order in time is L3.1. Groups or repeated measurements are L3.2 and L1.4. "A common total, a common denominator" is almost verbatim the first B-3 tag of §1. Many variables relative to units is L1.3. The checklist then says "Keep every dependence in both the plus and the minus world", which is exactly what makes these errors manifest in `minus`. Since it goes only to B-2 and B-3 briefs, it comes close to telling the author what to plant. "Not told the error" is literally true, but misleading unless the claim names what the checklist contains.
- **Fix:** Allowed claim for W-1 in I-D4: "... with worlds declared by a model of a different Claude line that was not told the error, but was given a checklist naming the dependence kinds that B-2 and B-3 errors exploit (time order, grouped or repeated units, shared components including common denominators and totals, many variables) and told to keep them in the minus world." Delete "It is the same for every class" as support for the checklist being generic: there are only two classes, and both are targeted. Alternatively, test genericity with decoys (R1-7). ### R1-7 (major): Only B-2/B-3 briefs reach the world authors; W-0 against W-1 is not specified as a matched comparison

- **Passage:** "each arm uses fresh agents of the PC model line, the briefs are shuffled, and the agents of the two arms are disjoint."
- **Reason:** (i) Every brief is B-2 or B-3: either prediction accuracy on new data, or an association after accounting for a named quantity. ### R1-8 (major): Repair and drop leave forking paths and are not frozen before the worlds

- **Section:** §2.4; §3 item 5; §1 Reserve
- **Passage:** "The criterion author runs V1–V3. A pair that fails gets one repair: the size is raised within the limits; or the model is moved to the source's family; or the brief is rewritten with the same claim type."
- **Reason:** These are forking paths. The agent that chooses the repair has just seen the faulty and fixed rates. Which of the three repairs to use, how far to raise the size, and what "same claim type" allows are all left open. A model change or a brief rewrite means new criteria written after the faulty criterion's behaviour was seen. PB and PC forbade running the faulty criterion before the freeze to prevent exactly that kind of tuning. The threshold is protected only for V2 ("never moved to make V2 pass"), so it may move to make V1 pass. The draft does not say who rewrites the brief, or whether the term check, the "what a brief may state" check and a new reference world follow. The model repair contradicts §2.1: if the source names a family, the twins already use it. Nothing limits informal runs of validity_check.py before the logged one, and the validity stage is not frozen before the world authors start. Reserves are not matched to subtype or tag, so drops can shift a class toward subtypes whose errors manifest easily.
- **Fix:** Replace with a decision table: "V1 fails: the size is raised once to the smaller of the source size and the cap; if it is already there, the pair is dropped. V2 fails: drop. V3 fails: one repair of the criterion bodies by the same author, then drop. A repair never changes the claim, its statistic, its threshold or the model; briefs are not rewritten." The designer, not the criterion author, runs validity_check.py. The script appends every run (SHA-256 of the inputs, seeds, counts) to a log, and a run missing from the log is a protocol breach. All runs, repairs, drops and substitutions are frozen in FREEZE_VALIDITY.json before any world author starts. Reserves are drawn in a fixed order within the same subtype (B-2) or tag (B-3); a class whose subtype or tag minimum cannot be met is reported as such.

### R1-10 (major): Nothing constrains or checks the reference world

- **Section:** §2.2; §3 item 4; §2.1 private note
- **Passage:** "A fresh structure author is told the error, the source and the private note. It writes reference.py: a plus world (claim true) and a minus world (claim false), each with the structure at the value in the private note."

### R1-11 (major): No rule for attrition: reserves can run out and n can fall below 20

- **Section:** §1 Reserve; §6 H-D1/H-D2; §6 H-D3
- **Passage:** "Every non-dropped pair counts (20 per class)." / "That is at least 16 of 20 on both."
- **Reason:** There are only 6 reserves per class, used only for §2.4 drops, and reserves can fail validity too. The validity audit judged 22 of the 46 Phase B and C pairs adequate. If about a quarter of Phase D pairs still drop after one repair, the reserves are likely to run out. This is the step most likely to fail, and nothing handles it: there is no minimum n, no bar for n below 20 and no "insufficient cases" rule. Drops at the skeleton check, at the fidelity audit (part B) or for timing are arm-specific, which breaks H-D3's pairing.
- **Fix:** "The bar is the one-sided 95% Wilson lower bound ≥ 0.6 at the actual n: 16 of 20; 15 of 19 or 18; 14 of 17; 13 of 16. A class with fewer than 16 pairs is 'insufficient cases' and has no verdict. Reserves replace drops of §2.4 and of fidelity part A before item 6, and are validated with the same repair rule; reserve use is reported. A skeleton that stays invalid, a part-B 'no' or a timeout in one arm counts as a miss and a failed clean pass in that arm (intention to treat); H-D3 uses every pair that entered item 6." If attrition this high is expected, raise the reserve to 10 per class.

### R1-12 (major): V2 admits fixed criteria that sit at the gate's flag threshold

- **Section:** §2.3 V2
- **Passage:** "at most 0.0808 of reference minus samples; at most 0.0808 of the gate's noise-probe samples"
- **Reason:** 0.0808 of 200 means at most 16 holds. That is a point estimate at the edge of the gate's flag threshold, and the threshold itself moves with the Bonferroni family. I assumed the flag is a one-sided Wilson lower bound above 0.05 at level 0.05/m, which reproduces the audit's "a flag needs 18". Under that assumption the first flagged count is 16 for m = 1, 17 for m = 4, 18 for m = 6 to 10, and 19 for m = 12 to 20. A fixed criterion whose true `minus` rate is 0.08 passes V2 with probability 0.57, and is then flagged in an independent 200-sample stream with probability 0.34. At a true rate of 0.07 the figures are 0.76 and 0.17. V2 therefore admits fixed criteria that often fail clean pass even in perfect worlds. That biases clean pass downward in every arm, and the power figures of §6 do not allow for it.
- **Fix:** "V2: on 1000 reference minus samples and 1000 noise-probe samples, drawn from a seed stream disjoint from the gate's, the fixed criterion holds in at most 0.065, and in at least 0.70 of 1000 plus samples." A valid test at the 0.05 level passes this with high probability; a criterion with a true rate of 0.08 fails it.

### R1-13 (major): The new sizes contradict W-0's "unchanged" instructions, and the timing rule does not bind the W arms

- **Section:** §2.1 Sizes; §3 item 6
- **Passage:** "At most 2000 units along any axis and at most 2 000 000 values in any array. The timing limit is 30 minutes per check with the fixed criterion" / "W-0 gets PC's world instructions unchanged"
- **Reason:** PC's world instructions (rule 8) and its frozen check_skeletons.py reject any dimension above 1000 and any array above 500 000 values, so "unchanged" contradicts the new sizes. A timeout counts as in R1-11 for H-D1 and H-D2, and as "not comparable" (excluded) for H-D3. State a compute budget and what happens if it is exceeded.

### R1-16 (major): The structure statistic may not be computable, and the miss labels misattribute

- **Section:** §2.1 private note; §5 Structure measured in the declared worlds
- **Passage:** "It is reported as the mean and as the share of samples at or above half the source value. Each miss gets a mechanical label: 'declared worlds lack the structure' when that share is below 0.5; otherwise 'structure present, not flagged'."
- **Reason:** Some structure statistics cannot be computed from the brief's data keys. A shared measurement-error term in a denominator cannot be observed from the measured components alone, so "the correlation induced by a shared term" needs latent values that the W worlds do not return. It also covers pairs that are flagged but not attributable, which count as misses under §5 although the gate flagged them.

### R1-17 (major): Power rests on unargued rates, and H-D3 ignores that noise-probe detections cannot differ between arms

- **Section:** §6 Power and H-D3 table; §5 Run
- **Passage:** "Power: at true rates of 0.85 on both, the class passes with probability 0.69; at 0.90, with 0.92." and the H-D3 power table
- **Reason:** The arithmetic is right. P(X ≥ 16 | n = 20, p = 0.85) is 0.830, squared 0.689; at 0.90 the joint power is 0.916; with 16 pairs it is 0.624. The exact McNemar powers at n = 40 are 0.691, 0.956 and 0.485. The assumptions, however, are not argued. Phase C's PB-style detection was 7 of 12 for B-2 and 8 of 10 for B-3, and the attributable counts would be lower. Clean pass will be pushed down by R1-4 and R1-12. At 0.80 on both rates the joint power is 0.40. For H-D3: the noise probe is generated from the shapes and the gate's seeds, so it is the same in every arm, and a pair attributable through the noise stream is concordant by construction. In PC, 11 of the 15 B-2/B-3 detections were generic. ## Overall judgment

Worth running once the blocking and major findings are fixed, with a narrower claim. Validating each pair before the run, scoring only detections the error explains, and measuring the structure in the declared worlds address the real defects that the Phase B and C audits found. The W-0/W-1 contrast is also a clean single-change comparison, once both arms get matched batches and decoys. As written, though, the design cannot deliver the separation it promises. Arm R is selected by V1/V2 and so cannot serve as a ceiling (R1-1). The primary endpoint is defined in two incompatible ways (R1-2). Clean-pass failures under the checklist would be misread as gate false alarms (R1-4). Even after the fixes, the confirmatory result will mostly measure whether a prompted world author plants the structure the error needs; the gate's own share shows only through the noise-probe split. The claims should say that. The step most likely to fail is attrition at V1/V2 exhausting six reserves per class, so the n and reserve rules of R1-11 are needed before the freeze. A targeted second review of the revised §2 to §5, §8 and the three tools is advisable.
